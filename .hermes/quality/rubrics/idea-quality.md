# Idea Quality Rubric

## Purpose

Evaluate whether a raw idea is clear enough to enter discovery.

## Required Inputs

- Idea brief
- User or market context
- Assumptions
- Open questions

## Quality Dimensions

| Dimension | What To Check |
| --- | --- |
| Clarity | The idea can be stated in one sentence. |
| User relevance | A target user or customer is named. |
| Problem signal | The idea points to a real pain or opportunity. |
| Timing | There is a reason to explore it now. |
| Next-step readiness | Discovery questions are clear. |

## 5-Point Scoring Criteria

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Vague concept with no user, problem, or outcome. |
| 2 | Interesting direction, but user and problem are unclear. |
| 3 | Clear enough to discuss, with some assumptions unresolved. |
| 4 | Clear opportunity with user, problem, outcome, and questions. |
| 5 | Sharp opportunity brief with evidence, constraints, and discovery path. |

## Pass/Fail Threshold

Pass at 4 or higher. Fail if the user, problem, or desired outcome is missing.

## Common Failure Patterns

- Describes a feature before the user pain.
- Uses broad markets like "everyone" or "all teams".
- Has no evidence or reason to prioritize now.
- Treats a technology preference as a product idea.

## Improvement Guidance

Rewrite the idea as: "For `<user>`, solve `<problem>` so they can `<outcome>`." Add assumptions and discovery questions.

## Example Of A High-Quality Output

"For course operations managers, reduce manual weekly course status reporting by consolidating readiness, learner progress, and issue signals into one workspace. Evidence comes from repeated spreadsheet reconciliation and delayed learner risk discovery."
