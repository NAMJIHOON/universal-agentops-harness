# Marketing Quality Rubric

## Purpose

Evaluate whether marketing strategy and copy are specific, testable, and aligned with the product.

## Required Inputs

- Marketing brief
- Customer segment
- Value proposition
- Competitor comparison
- Landing page copy

## Quality Dimensions

| Dimension | What To Check |
| --- | --- |
| Segment fit | Message targets a clear audience. |
| Pain alignment | Copy reflects real user pain. |
| Differentiation | USP explains why this alternative matters. |
| Testability | Campaign hypotheses and metrics are explicit. |
| Claim safety | Claims are supportable and not misleading. |

## 5-Point Scoring Criteria

| Score | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Generic copy with no segment or pain. |
| 2 | Some message direction, but USP or channel is weak. |
| 3 | Usable marketing brief with testable gaps. |
| 4 | Clear positioning, USP, channels, and experiment metrics. |
| 5 | Strong message system tied to segment, proof, differentiation, and validation. |

## Pass/Fail Threshold

Pass at 4 or higher. Fail if the audience or core message is unclear.

## Common Failure Patterns

- Uses broad promises like "save time" without proof.
- Confuses product features with benefits.
- Ignores competitors and substitutes.
- Lacks campaign hypotheses.

## Improvement Guidance

Tie headline, benefits, proof points, channels, and experiments to the priority segment and pain.

## Example Of A High-Quality Output

"Core message: 'Run online courses with earlier risk signals and fewer manual status reports.' It targets course operations managers and tests landing page conversion from education operations newsletters."
